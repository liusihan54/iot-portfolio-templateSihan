One difficulty in this module was interpreting the LDR measurements. When the lamp was switched off, the output was still approximately 1.30 V, close to the readings measured with the lamp at larger distances. This showed that ambient light was affecting the experiment, so lamp distance alone could not reliably represent the light reaching the sensor. Although the linear model gave a smaller error at the validation point, I could not conclude that the LDR itself was linear. Next time, I would use a dark enclosure and keep the lamp and sensor alignment fixed.

The potentiometer experiment also helped me distinguish measurement stability from accuracy. At the independent validation point, two-point calibration reduced the voltage error from approximately −0.156 V to −0.028 V, but it did not eliminate it. I learned that stable readings and more decimal places do not necessarily mean an accurate result. Before applying further corrections, I would test more independent voltage points to understand where the remaining error comes from.
